Executive Summary

Checking 16 career sites by hand for entry-level SDR, BDR and analyst roles in the Bay Area takes time every week, and most postings are wrong on level or location. This pipeline scrapes them (Workday and Greenhouse APIs where they exist, a browser for the rest), filters in plain Python on title, seniority, stated years of experience and Bay Area location, remembers what it has already shown you, and emails an HTML digest with a "new since last run" section.

A real dry run on 2026-10-20 took under 3 minutes and found 5 open roles: 4 business development roles at Salesforce and a Sales Development Representative role at LinkedIn, all in San Francisco. Their pay ranges came from the postings themselves, and I checked one against the live Salesforce page. Uber and SAP blocked the scraper.

How pay is labeled

When a posting states a range, the digest shows it as is. Salesforce lists several ranges by location, and the scraper takes the first one. When a posting has no range, a local model estimates one from the role and your resume, and the digest marks it "(estimated)". None needed estimating on this run.

02Filtering is code, the model only fills gaps

Location, title keywords (including the many names for the same entry-level job: SDR, BDR, ADR, MDR, inside sales, BI analyst...), seniority words (Senior, Staff, Lead, Manager...) and required years of experience are all deterministic rules in src/utils/filters.py, covered by tests. A posting that never states required years is kept rather than dropped, and "preferred" or "even better if" years don't count as requirements. The CrewAI agents only write the digest summary and pay estimates, so a model mistake can't hide a matching job.

03The model invented salaries in its summary, so now its numbers are checked

The digest opens with a short summary the model writes. In two of four dry runs it claimed "estimated salary ranges" like $65K-$80K, even though every job had an employer-stated range and nothing was estimated. The tables were right; the prose was made up. Now any number in the summary that isn't in the digest data (job fields, totals, per-company counts) gets the summary rejected, and the digest falls back to a plain one-liner. The check caught the next invented set too; in the final run the model's summary only used real numbers and was kept.

Same lesson as the other agent projects

A small model will add plausible numbers to prose. The fix is the same everywhere: compare every figure it writes against the data it was given.

04What it can't see

Uber and SAP answered with 403 Forbidden, and SAP's browser fallback timed out. Corporate bot detection blocks some scrapers, so a company with zero results is logged with a warning instead of being silently treated as "no openings". Workday searches cover one office city per company for now.
